OVERVIEW

Rebuilding for trust, clarity, and performance

The result: 17× lighter, 2.6× lower bounce rate, and 21% more engagement with key information.
MemeCore is a Web3 startup building a sustainable meme coin ecosystem around M. Its original website relied heavily on flashy illustrations and animations to stand out in the crowded crypto market. After launch, however, the visual-heavy experience made it difficult for investors and partners to quickly assess the company’s credibility, while its 48.6 MB size created accessibility barriers especially for users with slower internet connections.

As the sole product designer and product manager, I redesigned the website to balance brand experience, trust, information clarity, and performance—restructuring the information architecture and reducing the site to 2.8 MB. I also introduced vibe coding into the workflow and documented the process, which later evolved into a designer–developer workflow across the company.

ABOUT MEMECORE

A web3 company building trusted meme coins

MemeCore is a Web3 startup behind M, a community-driven meme coin built to address the lack of trust in meme coins. Beyond the coin itself, MemeCore builds platforms and creates online and offline experiences that connect the M community and drive lasting engagement.

SITE BACKGROUND

Building credibility became more important after MemeCore’s major exchange listings

Early on, MemeCore used flashy GIF illustrations to capture attention in the crowded Web3 market. This strategy helped grow its social community to 600K+ and service participation to 3.2M+ before launch. MemeCore later secured listings on major exchanges, built its own ecosystem and rewards programs, and formed partnerships with reputable institutions. As the company grew, communicating the project’s value and credibility became more important than simply capturing attention through visuals.

PROBLEM

Prospective investors and partners struggled to understand the project and find the information they needed

Despite MemeCore’s growth, market skepticism remained due to its origins as a meme coin. The Growth & Marketing team frequently heard from prospective investors and partners that they still didn’t understand what MemeCore was or where it was headed. Limited information and unclear content hierarchy made key information difficult to find, requiring the team to repeatedly explain the project during external events and IR meetings.

DATA ANALYSIS & CHALLENGES

Unclear content structure and heavy page weight
drove a 56.1% initial bounce rate

As the PM and product designer, I tackled both design and development challenges. Analysis of six months of Google Analytics and Clarity data showed that many users left the first page without taking action or dropped off before it finished loading.
I focused on improving user experience and web performance together.

DESIGN

Hover-dependent navigation and unclear visual cues

The first screen emphasized graphics without navigation or key information, making it unclear where to start. Users had to discover pages through an unfamiliar hover interaction, leading to drop-off before reaching key content.

DEVELOPMENT

16× heavier than the average webpage

While the average desktop webpage is around 3MB, MemeCore’s site was 48.6MB, requiring an extra loading screen before users could access the site. This created greater friction in regions with limited network infrastructure, making optimization essential.
