OVERVIEW

Moving key information upfront and cutting site weight by 17×

The result: 17× lighter, 2.6× lower bounce rate, and 21% more engagement with key information.
MemeCore is a Web3 startup building a sustainable meme coin ecosystem around M. Its visually bold website helped the company stand out, but as MemeCore grew, investors and partners struggled to understand its value and credibility. Heavy assets also caused long load times, preventing some users from entering the site.

As the sole product designer and product manager, I redesigned the website to balance brand identity, information clarity, and performance. I also took the initiative to introduce vibe coding, which later evolved into a company-wide designer–developer workflow.

ABOUT  MEMECORE

A Web3 company building
an ecosystem around M coin

MemeCore is a Web3 startup behind M, a community-driven meme coin built to address trust issues in the meme coin market. Beyond the coin, MemeCore builds platforms and experiences that connect its community and drive long-term engagement.

THE IMPORTANCE OF SITE

Company growth shifted the website from visual impact to clear information

Early on, MemeCore used flashy GIF illustrations to stand out in the crowded Web3 market, helping grow its community to 600K+ and service participation to 3.2M+ before the M token launched. As MemeCore grew through major exchange listings, the website needed to shift from capturing attention to communicating value and building credibility.

PROBLEM

Investors and partners struggled to understand MemeCore and its direction

Despite MemeCore’s growth, skepticism around meme coins remained. Investors and partners repeatedly told the Growth & Marketing team that MemeCore and its direction were unclear. Reviewing the site revealed limited key information and no clear hierarchy, forcing the team to repeatedly explain the project in meetings.

DATA ANALYSIS & CHALLENGES

Unclear content structure and heavy page weight
drove a 56.1% initial bounce rate

Six months of Google Analytics and Microsoft Clarity data showed that users either left the first page without taking action or dropped off before it finished loading. As the PM and product designer, I tackled both design and development challenges, improving user experience and web performance together.

PROBLEM 1

Hidden navigation and no clear starting point

The first screen showed only graphics, with no navigation or key information. Pages could only be found through an unfamiliar hover interaction, so users dropped off before reaching key content.

PROBLEM 2

Oversized images and videos slowing the site

At 48.6MB, the site was 16× heavier than an average desktop page and needed an extra loading screen. On weaker networks, this made the site hard to even open, so optimization was essential.

INSIGHT

Users stayed 5× longer once they reached detail pages

The content itself was worth reading, but many users left before ever reaching it. As the business strategy became clearer after launch, I saw an opportunity to surface key information earlier and make it easier to navigate.

GOAL

Help investors quickly find key information,
increase visits to key content, and reduce early drop-off.

HYPOTHESIS
PROBLEM 1

If we provide a clear landing page and business-focused navigation, users will reach key points easily

The original first screen only presented graphics, with no navigation or clear action to guide users. Providing a clear starting point would make the content easier to discover.

PROBLEM 2

If we replace heavy GIFs with CSS animations and static WebP images, we can reduce page size and loading drop-off

Rather than removing motion altogether, I looked for lighter ways to preserve the site’s visual character.

DESIGN IMPROVEMENT 1

Building global navigation around user needs and evolving business priorities

The original website had no navigation bar, leaving key information scattered across individual pages. To build a clear information hierarchy, I analyzed users’ most frequent questions, MemeCore’s business model, and other Web3 services such as Sui, Monad, and Pudgy Penguins to identify common information priorities and navigation patterns.

I introduced the first navigation bar, then evolved it through two further iterations. The first version established the initial structure. The second consolidated related content and provided direct access to $M. The third responded to MemeCore’s shift toward offline events and community growth by surfacing announcements, community updates, and partner resources.

FIRST RELEASE

The initial focus was on bringing information into one place. However, over time, some Web3 contents and pages became outdated, while related content remained scattered across separate menu items.

SECOND RELEASE

I removed unnecessary items, consolidated related content into shared categories, and added a Buy $M button linking to exchange information.

FINAL RELEASE

As the company shifted toward a community strategy centered on online and offline events, I added relevant pages and redesigned multiple screens to support the new direction.

DESIGN IMPROVEMENT 2

Turning a visual-first homepage into an information-first landing page

Previously, users had to rely on hover interactions to discover individual pages. Working with Growth and Marketing teams, I identified users’ most common questions and the company’s key messages, prioritized them by importance, and structured the new landing page around them with clear paths to relevant detail pages.

BEFORE

Information was embedded in an illustration-led experience, requiring users to hover over buildings to discover pages and making key content difficult to scan.

AFTER

Business strategy and the company's key projects were surfaced in order of importance, with clear paths to relevant detail pages.

TRADE-OFF

Keeping the brand distinctive while making information clearer

The original site relied heavily on graphics, which often competed with key information. When I proposed prioritizing clarity, the illustration team worried that simplifying the experience could weaken MemeCore’s identity. Rather than reducing illustrations, I aligned the team around a more intentional approach: scroll interactions to capture attention at key moments, and content-driven illustrations to improve understanding without losing the brand.

1. Engagement zones: use interaction where exploration adds value

I used scroll interactions selectively where we needed to draw users in or redirect their attention.

Opening: scroll interactions draw users into the experience and encourage them to continue beyond the opening section.

$M exchange: A second interaction re-engages attention around $M exchange, a business-critical part of the site.

2. Information zones: use interaction where exploration adds value

In information-heavy sections, I removed unnecessary interactions and paired each key message with a single, relevant illustration.
The more restrained visuals reduced distraction and strengthened information hierarchy while preserving MemeCore’s distinctive brand.

I also used consistent layouts across sections so users could scan and understand information more easily.

DESIGN RESULT

I compared the first three months with a three-month post-launch period with similar traffic levels. After redesigning the landing page as a starting point for exploration, more users reached key information and continued to relevant detail pages.

SHIPPING PROCESS
NEW CHALLNEGE

17% visitors showed rage or dead clicks, while site performance scored 55

Although we had already reduced the size of the GIF assets, performance issues remained after launch. Clarity showed that approximately 17% of visitors repeatedly triggered rage or dead clicks, while session replays revealed that users on slower networks struggled to view the page smoothly. A Lighthouse performance score of 55 confirmed that further optimization was needed.

EXECUTION STRATEGY

Bridging the development gap with vibe coding

Adding to the challenge, our frontend developer was reassigned to another urgent initiative midway through the project. Rather than pause the project or reduce its scope, I took ownership of implementation through vibe coding. I connected Claude Code with Figma API and GitLab to build the frontend and later handled some backend work. By testing and refining the design directly in the browser, I reduced handoffs and rework.

IMPLEMENTATION DECISION

Replacing full-scene GIFs with static webP and selective CSS motion

I separated each full-scene GIF into a static WebP background and key animated assets, using CSS to animate only the key elements. Information-focused sections became fully static. This reduced page weight while preserving the intended visual experience, and the lighter site no longer required a loading screen.

SHIPPING RESULT

Improving site performance and development efficiency

The optimization increased the Lighthouse performance score from 55 to 92, reduced page weight by 94%, and lowered loading drop-off from 24% to 6%. Beyond product performance, the vibe-coding workflow reduced development and QA time for navigation and page updates from about one month to five days. I documented the process from a designer’s perspective and shared it across the company, where it was recognized as an internal best practice.
