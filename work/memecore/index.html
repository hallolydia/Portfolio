OVERVIEW

As MemeCore grew, its website had to earn investors' trust

MemeCore is a Web3 startup behind M, a community-driven meme coin. After major exchange listings and a top-20 market cap, its website became the first place investors and partners checked.

As the sole product designer and PM, I led the IA, UX, and implementation with engineers and illustrators, and shipped the final build myself with AI coding agents.

PROBLEM

The site left investors unsure what MemeCore stood for

Flashy GIF art had grown the community to 600K+, but it didn't explain the project. With little key information and no clear hierarchy, the Growth & Marketing team had to explain MemeCore in every meeting.

Key Feedbacks

  • “I saw the site, but still don’t get what MemeCore does.”- Prospective Partner

  • “It looks well-funded, but it’s still a meme coin.”- MemeCore Investor

  • “The strategy feels focused on short-term price.”- Crypto VC

DATA ANALYSIS & CHALLENGES

Unclear structure and heavy pages
drove a 56.1% initial bounce rate

Six months of Google Analytics and Clarity data showed where users left; a usability test with 5 people showed why. As both PM and designer, I took on both, from design to code.

PROBLEM 1

Hidden navigation and no clear starting point

The first screen showed only graphics, and pages were hidden behind a hover interaction. In testing, 3 of 5 couldn’t find the roadmap.

PROBLEM 2

Oversized images and videos slowing the site

At 48.6MB, the site was 16× heavier than an average page. Clarity showed 24% of visitors left before it finished loading.

INSIGHT

Users stayed 5× longer once they reached detail pages

The content was worth reading, but most users left before reaching it. The job was to surface it earlier.

GOAL

Help investors reach key information faster:
+15% detail-page visits, −10% loading drop-off.

HYPOTHESIS
FOR PROBLEM 1

If the landing page and navigation follow investors' questions, they'll reach key information faster.

FOR PROBLEM 2

If heavy GIFs become WebP and CSS motion, pages will load faster and fewer people will leave.

DESIGN SOLUTION 1

A navigation built around investors' questions and the business

Users' frequent questions set what came first, the business model set the order, and Web3 services like Sui and Monad set familiar patterns.

DESIGN SOLUTION 2

From a visual-first map to an information-first landing page

With Growth and Marketing, I ranked investors' top questions and our key messages, then ordered the page by them.

BEFORE

Pages were hidden inside an illustrated map and appeared only on hover, making key content hard to find.

AFTER

Key projects and business messages appear in order of importance, each linked to its detail page.

TRADE-OFF

Turning the illustration team's pushback into a shared design rule

I reframed the debate from aesthetics to business impact, and the team agreed on one rule: interaction for attention, simple illustrations for understanding.

Me

"Simpler visuals will make the information much clearer."

Illustrators

"But it could weaken MemeCore's core identity."

Me

"A beautiful site means nothing if users can't get in.
Let's keep the illustrations, but give each one a clear job."

01 Scroll interactions that guide attention

I used scroll interactions only at moments where we needed to hold their attention.

Opening: Scroll motion draws users into the story so they keep scrolling past the first screen.

$M exchange: Brings attention back to the $M exchange section, a business-critical part of the site.

02 Illustrations that support understanding

In information sections, one consistent layout lets illustrations support the content, so users scan faster.

Three months before vs. after, with similar traffic: fewer users left right away, and more reached detail pages.

NEW CHALLENGE

The site was still slow, and our frontend
developer was reassigned, so I built it myself

Lighthouse scored 55 and 17% of visitors rage-clicked on unresponsive pages. Rather than pause, I connected Claude Code to the Figma API and GitLab and took over the frontend.

IMPLEMENTATION DECISION

Swapping full-scene GIFs for WebP and CSS motion

Each scene was one heavy GIF, so I kept motion only where it mattered and made the rest static. The loading screen was no longer needed.

01  Keeping the motion by animating only key assets

BEFORE

The whole scene was a single GIF, so it couldn't be optimized further.

AFTER

A static, compressed
WebP background.

Each key asset cut out and animated in CSS.

02  Making information sections static, where clarity mattered more

SHIPPING RESULT

Vibe coding cut updates from a month to 5 days, and performance rose 55→92

Page weight fell 94% and loading drop-off went from 24% to 6%. I documented the workflow, and it became the company's internal best practice.

LEARNING

Ownership drove results; next time, I'll plan around the team's capacity

I led the project from planning to launch ahead of schedule and earned the company's highest performance rating. But I took the team's yes as capacity and left no buffer for urgent requests. Next time, I'll check each person's workload and plan for the unexpected.
