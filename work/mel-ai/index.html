OVERVIEW

Turning a promising AI interaction into a discoverable, engaging experience

Match Group is a global leader in dating and relationship technology, with platforms including Tinder, Hinge, and Match. This project explored an AI dating experience that helps users build meaningful, ongoing relationships with AI companions.

I designed ‘Interaction Mode’ to offer richer interactions beyond casual conversation. Behavioral data from 1,000+ users revealed two challenges: low discoverability during conversations and low engagement after entering the mode. I redesigned the experience to help users discover Interaction Mode more easily and stay engaged longer, driving a 5× increase in Interaction Mode starts and a 5.5× increase in session duration.

CONTEXT
ABOUT MATCH GROUP

The world’s leading dating company behind Tinder and Hinge

Match Group is the #1 global online dating company behind brands like Tinder and Hinge. Its portfolio serves different relationship needs and audiences, with a shared mission to help people build meaningful connections through technology.

SERVICE BACKGROUND

Exploring AI video dating to address Tinder users’ dating fatigue

Tinder’s growth slowed after losing 100,000 subscribers following the pandemic. Users also felt burdened by the time and effort required to explore potential partners and reach a meaningful match. Meanwhile, the AI character chat market was growing rapidly in North America. Our research found users treating AI characters as romantic partners, even on platforms not designed for dating.

This revealed an opportunity to bring these relationships beyond text. We created Mel, an AI video dating service that brings users’ ideal partners to life through realistic video interactions.

BEHAVIORAL INSIGHT

Longer conversations weren't more realistic, they were more imaginative

To validate the potential of a conversation-based subscription model, we tested whether users could sustain 10-minute AI video calls. However, the average conversation lasted only half of the target time. with many users saying they did not know what to talk about.

We then analyzed users who talked for over 30 minutes and found that 90.1% used role-play and scenarios to continue their conversations. Based on this insight, we introduced Interactive Mode.

THE CHALLENGE

Unclear guidance resulted in only 13.3% of users entering Interactive Mode

Our team’s vision was to create a natural, seamless AI conversation, so we initially used dialogue-only entry into Interactive Mode rather than UI guidance. However, many users failed to recognize the feature, resulting in an entry rate of only 13.3%. Users who entered Interactive Mode stayed longer, suggesting that the bigger barrier was getting users into the experience.

GOAL

Increase Interactive Mode entry to 50% and extend conversation time

As Interactive Mode was both the service’s core feature and the foundation of its business model, our primary goal was to help at least half of call users enter the mode. Our secondary goal was to extend conversation time through continued interaction.

EXPLORATION

Aligning product direction, user experience, and technical feasibility

Working with PM, design, and ML/engineering, we aligned user needs with product goals and technical constraints to define three design directions.

01 ENTRY

Make entry visible without disrupting the conversation

Product need
Surface Interactive Mode as soon as the call begins.

UX consideration
Keep entry natural instead of requiring exact commands.

Technical constraint
Avoid interaction patterns that require heavy implementation.

→ Persistent entry + contextual prompts

02 ENGAGEMENT

Reduce the effort to keep conversations going

Product need
Give users reasons to continue after entering.

UX consideration
Help users decide what scenario to start and what to say next.

Technical capability

→ Scenario options + suggested replies

03 CONTINUITY

Turn long-term memory into a user-facing advantage

Market signal
AI chat users frustrated when conversation context was lost during longer interactions.

Technical capability
MEL could retain previous conversation context through long-term memory.

UX opportunity
Make that continuity visible so users could easily return to previous scenarios.

→ Interactive Mode history

DESIGN DECISION : ENTRY POINT

Making Interactive Mode easier to discover
without disrupting the conversation

To balance seamlessness with discoverability, we added clear entry points without disrupting the conversation: a persistent button for direct access and contextual prompts during relevant dialogue.

DESIGN EXPLORATION

Making Hidden Features Discoverable

Hiding the feature preserved the conversation, but made it easy to miss. I explored how to surface it without disrupting the flow.

OPTION A

Hidden behind an extra step

Users had to tap an unfamiliar icon before discovering the two modes.

OPTION B

Too disruptive

The prominent prompt made the feature clear, but competed with the conversation.

SELECTED DIRECTION

Curiosity-driven CTA

An animated, branded CTA invited exploration without interrupting the conversation.

DESIGN DECISION: CONVERSATIONAL ENGAGEMENT

Reducing the effort to start and sustain a scenario

Describing a desired scenario through conversation was unfamiliar UX to users, making it difficult to get started. We introduced two ways to start and supported both high-intent users and users who needed inspiration.

DESIGN DECISION: CONVERSATIONAL ENGAGEMENT

Helping users keep the conversation moving

Even after choosing a scenario, users could pause while deciding what to say next. We added context-aware suggested replies to give users an easy starting point and keep conversation flowing.

Helping users return to past conversations with context

After users left Interactive Mode, there was no clear way to return to a previous scenario. We introduced My Conversation, which automatically saves past sessions with the latest image and an AI-generated summary, making it easy for users to recognize and revisit their previous experiences.

LESSONS LEARNED

Using data to uncover user needs behind unexpected responses

We initially assumed users wanted AI conversations to feel like real video calls, so we designed a minimal interface around that experience. However, usage remained below expectations, while behavioral data showed that users with longer conversations were more drawn to imaginative scenarios beyond everyday reality.

This taught us that in an emerging market without established mental models, design decisions must be guided by observed behavior rather than assumptions. Although time constraints prevented formal A/B testing, future iterations should validate user responses systematically to inform more impactful UX/UI decisions.
